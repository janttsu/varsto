All comparisons

Varsto, restic and BorgBackup

restic and BorgBackup (borg) are backup programs: they take snapshots of directories into an encrypted, deduplicated repository and restore them later. They are not sync tools and do not try to be. Varsto does both sync and backups of its own, and its restic mirror lets restic back up a vault independently of Varsto.

How restic and borg work

restic is a single executable that runs without a server. restic backup takes a snapshot of a directory into a repository, transferring only the parts of files that changed; restic snapshots lists them, restic restore brings one back, and restic check verifies the repository. Repositories can live on a local disk or on many storage types, self-hosted and online, and everything in them is encrypted. borg is a deduplicating backup program as well: files are split into chunks and a chunk already in the repository is not stored again, data is encrypted on the client (AES-256 with HMAC-SHA256) and optionally compressed, repositories live on local disks or on any host reachable over SSH, and archives can be mounted as a file system to browse and restore. Neither keeps your devices in step: a backup runs at a time you choose, from one machine, into one repository, and the files on the machine are left as they were.

Which one fits

restic / borg

Choose restic or borg when what you need is backups: mature, encrypted, deduplicated snapshots of a machine, restorable with the tool alone, on a schedule you run yourself.

Varsto

Choose Varsto when your devices should stay in step as well, you want a record of where every copy is and policies that warn, and you want backups that nothing can change on the same storages. Keep restic as a second, independent line of defence through the mirror.

Varsto is alpha software: do not trust it with data you have no other copy of, and its cryptography has not been independently audited. The other products here are mature. Facts about them were checked from their own documentation in October 2026; prices and plans change often, so see their own pages for current figures.
