Measured against Resilio Sync (November 2026)

From docs/testing/resilio-vs-varsto-2026-11.md in the repository, rendered 2026-11-10. These documents describe the main branch; the released version is 0.0.1-alpha.16. Sections marked 'after 0.0.1-alpha.16' are not in a release yet.

One measurement on rented machines, made twice: is Varsto's peer-to-peer transfer more efficient than Resilio Sync's in some cases? The first run (2026-11-07) used the daily build 20261106-46adda3; the second (2026-11-08) the daily 20261108-c50f260, which changed the pull (local blocks are reused, readers serve finished files while they still pull, requests are shared by peer speed, a failed request rests a peer only briefly). Resilio Sync 2.8.1 ran on both days. The tables below put the three side by side; the raw data is in data/resilio-vs-varsto-2026-11.jsonl (11-07) and data/resilio-vs-varsto-2026-11-08.jsonl (11-08).

Short answer. On these machines Varsto pulls faster than Resilio Sync in every initial sync of large, compressible or duplicated data and over a slow lossy link, and sends a third of the bytes for text. The new build (c50f260) closed the gap that mattered most: after a small change the reader now fetches only the changed blocks (22 MB with the peer instead of 1 148 MB), a renamed directory moves without data, duplicates are reused from disk (half the bytes), and two readers can feed each other. What did not change: the time to propagate a change is still three to five times Resilio Sync's (the writer's own all-folders sync and the reader's re-hashing of its local blocks cost more than the transfer), the writer's service still needs several times Resilio Sync's memory, and on the 10 000 small files Resilio Sync is faster when it does not stall, which it did in one run of two on both days. Of the two problems found on 11-07, the STUN-off stall is gone and the back-off failure did not recur in 20 runs.

What the scenarios show

  1. Many small files. Unchanged for Varsto (44-45 s both days, 10 000 files from the peer; the new build fetched 9 474 distinct blocks and reused the 526 duplicate ones locally, 16 MB less on the wire). Resilio Sync is faster when it does not stall (25-30 s), but on both days one of its two runs took over five minutes: a single small file stayed in its download state with no traffic (f002953 on 11-07, f003617 on 11-08, both about 27 KB) until Resilio Sync retried it some four and a half minutes later. Two stalls in four runs is not an accident; it is how 2.8.1 behaves with this folder.
  2. Two large files. Varsto 43-47 s against Resilio Sync's 109-110 s, both days alike. Nothing to reuse, so the new build is the same.
  3. Compressible text. Varsto 17-21 s and 170-175 MB on the wire for 537 MB of files, against 38-63 s and 563 MB: Varsto compresses every block with zstd before encrypting it and serves the same compressed, encrypted objects to peers; Resilio Sync sends the files as they are.
  4. Duplicate content. The clearest gain of the new build: 408 MB of the 816 MB were taken from blocks already on the reader's disk, 420 MB crossed the wire instead of 836 MB, and the pull took 12-14 s instead of 20-29 s. Resilio Sync sends every copy (857 MB, 78 s).
  5. Two readers at once. In the new build the readers can serve each other while they pull, and in one of the two runs they did: Warsaw got 0.67 GB of its 2.2 GB from Amsterdam, the writer sent 3.8 GB instead of 4.3 GB, and Amsterdam, uploading while it downloaded, took 90 s instead of 57 s. In the other run nothing went between the readers (the writer sent 4.4 GB, Amsterdam took 54 s). Warsaw finished in 95-101 s either way. Resilio Sync's readers exchanged 0.2-0.8 GB in each run, its writer sent 3.4 GB, and both readers took 96-108 s. On time the two are about even here; on the writer's upload Resilio Sync spreads the load more reliably.
  6. A small change after the sync (1 MiB rewritten in a 1 GiB file, 10 MiB appended to a 128 MiB log). This is what the new build set out to fix, and on bytes it did: the reader fetched 4 blocks (21-22 MB with peers, 38 MB on the interface with the bucket's metadata) and reused 1.19 GB of its own blocks, where the old build fetched the two files whole (1148 MB). Resilio Sync sends 12 MB. On time Varsto is still behind: 47 and 76 s against Resilio Sync's 11-13 s. Both halves cost: the writer's part (27-30 s from the change to the end of the push) is mostly its own watcher's sync of all five folders, which started two seconds after the change and took 25 s (the mut push itself took 3.5 s and uploaded 20 MB); the reader's part (15-46 s) is the service start plus a pull that reads and hashes the 1.1 GiB it already has to find the blocks to reuse (about 11 s of pull in the first run; the second run's pull took 46 s, not explained).
  7. Directory with 1.13 GiB renamed. The new build moves the files without a byte of data: 0.1 MB with peers, 16-17 MB on the interface (metadata from the bucket), 1.22 GB reused, in 33-43 s (writer 17-28 s, again mostly the all-folders sync; reader 15-16 s). The old build fetched everything again (1146 MB, 50 s), and so does Resilio Sync (1282 MB, 59-67 s). The reader no longer leaves the old, empty directory behind.
  8. Constrained link (40 ms added delay, 1 % loss, 100 Mbit/s on the reader's incoming traffic), the 10 000 small files. Varsto 324-347 s both days against Resilio Sync's 594-609 s, with about the same bytes (Varsto 331-355 MB on the interface, 295-312 MB with the peer; Resilio Sync 321 MB). Varsto's route log shows TCP; which transport Resilio Sync used was not recorded. The new build changed nothing here.

Bug status after c50f260

  1. Pull stops part-way with STUN off: gone. The same configuration (p2p enable --no-stun --public <ip:port> on the reader) that stopped twice at 8 846 of 10 000 files on 11-07 completed on 11-08: 10 000 files, 9 474 blocks from the peer, 526 reused, every SHA-256 right, in 61 s (slower than the 42-47 s with STUN on; one run, so no more than a hint). The diagnostic is BENCH_VARSTO_NO_STUN=1 ... run --scenario files-nostun.
  2. A sync right after the writer restarted fetched nothing: not seen again. 20 Varsto runs on 11-08 (every scenario, both repetitions, the diagnostic), none failed and none reported an unavailable file. The driver still restarts the reader's service after the writer's, so this is "did not recur", not "cannot recur".
  3. Whole files sent again after a change or rename: fixed (scenarios 6 and 7 above). The remaining cost of a change is time, not bytes.
  4. Duplicates fetched twice: fixed (scenario 4; 526 of the 10 000 small files were reused too).
  5. Readers do not help each other: works, but not every time (scenario 5: 0.67 GB reader to reader in one run, nothing in the other), at the price of a slower first reader when it does.
  6. Still open: the writer's peak RSS (0.4-0.8 GiB against Resilio Sync's 0.07-0.25 GiB); the writer service syncing all five folders every 10-20 s for minutes after a change (its log on 11-08 shows sync start subject= "all folders" back to back, 7-25 s each), which is what made the change steps' writer time 17-30 s; the empty-directory leftover of 11-07 is gone.

Method

  • Same machines, same data, same direction. The writer in Paris holds the data; the readers pull it. One tool runs at a time (the other one's daemons are stopped on all three machines), runs alternate between the tools (Varsto first in repetition 1, Resilio Sync first in repetition 2), and before every initial sync the page cache is dropped on all three machines and the reader starts with an empty folder and a new device (Varsto: a newly joined device; Resilio Sync: a new storage directory).
  • Data (gen-data.py, 4.7 GiB in all, random content from the operating system): files 10 000 files, 294 MB, sizes log-normal around 16 KiB, 5 % copies of earlier files; pair 2 x 1 GiB incompressible; text 128 log-like text files, 537 MB; dup 92 files, 816 MB, every file twice (unique files in orig/, the same bytes under other names in copies/); mut a 1 GiB random file and a 128 MiB log in dir/, for the change and rename steps. Every machine has the writer's list of path, size and SHA-256 of every file.
  • Varsto runs in its p2p mode as in p2p-bench.py: the vault's bucket (Scaleway Object Storage, fr-par) keeps only ledger, manifests and device records; every block is deleted from it before the runs (purge-chunks), so every block has to come from the writer, and a run where not every block came from a peer counts as failed. Settings on every device: rendezvous service off (so no relay through it), router port mapping off, update check off, automatic verification, bit-rot check, forwarding and mailbox off; STUN on (see "Varsto findings"). LAN discovery has no switch in Varsto; its multicast beacons do not leave a machine's network, and the three machines are in three countries. The time is POST /api/sync on the reader's running service, from the request to the answer, after the folder was attached; the reader's join, folder approval and service start are not timed. From the third large run on, the reader's service is also restarted after the writer's two restarts (finding 2 below); the earlier runs that passed are kept.
  • Resilio Sync runs from a configuration file (rslsync --nodaemon --config), one shared folder per data set on the writer (read-write secret) and on each reader the read-only secret of one folder, with use_relay_server, use_tracker, search_lan and use_sync_trash false, use_upnp false, a fixed listening_port and known_hosts with the writer's address (and the other reader's, with two readers). The writer indexed every folder once before the runs (21 s to idle); before each run it is started again and waited for until it uses no CPU and reads no disk for 5 s. The time runs from starting rslsync on the reader to the first poll (every 0.25 s) that sees every file at its size and no *.!sync temporary file; a SHA-256 check of every file follows (not timed), and the poll goes on if it fails. The raw data also has the time to 99 % of the bytes (bytes99); it was within 6 s of the completion in every run where it was recorded (from the second files run on).
  • Verification. Both tools' results count only when every file's SHA-256 equals the writer's list.
  • Wire bytes are counted at the reader: the received plus sent bytes of its public interface (/proc/net/dev) over the timed part, which for Varsto includes its requests to the bucket and for both a little SSH; "with peers" counts only the traffic with the other two machines (iptables counters by address).
  • CPU and memory of the daemon: user plus system CPU seconds over the timed part (/proc/<pid>/stat), peak RSS (VmHWM) at the end of it. The writer's peak RSS covers its life since its last start, which includes its start-up scan of all five folders (Varsto's writer service is restarted twice before each run, Resilio Sync's once).
  • Changes after the initial sync (set mut, after its initial sync, the reader's daemon still running): in the middle of dir/big.bin 1 MiB of new random bytes is written in place, and 10 MiB of log lines are appended to dir/app.log (11 MiB changed of 1.13 GiB); then dir is renamed to dir-moved. Resilio Sync: from the change on the writer to the reader's verified folder, its own detection of the change included (the writer's and reader's clocks agreed to within about 0.2 s, estimated over SSH and recorded with each run). Varsto: the writer's service is paused (it still serves blocks but does not push on its own), its POST /api/sync (the push) is timed, the bucket's new blocks are deleted (not timed), then the reader's POST /api/sync is timed; the time is the sum of the two. Varsto's own detection of the change and the reader's wait for its next sync are not in it.
  • Second run (2026-11-08), what changed in the procedure. In build c50f260 a paused service stops its peer-to-peer part, so nothing is paused any more, and folder attach on the running reader service starts the pull by itself. Varsto's time is therefore, like Resilio Sync's, from the moment the reader is told about the folder (POST /api/folder/attach) to the first poll (every 0.25 s) that sees every file at its size, confirmed by the SHA-256 check; its pull report (blocks from peers, blocks reused locally) is read from the service log. For the change and rename steps the reader's service is stopped while the writer publishes the change (the writer's time runs from the change to the end of its POST /api/sync, which its own watcher may already have started) and the bucket's new blocks are deleted; the reader's time is then from its service start to the complete folder. The writer's bytes sent to each reader (iptables) are recorded in every step. The reader's service is restarted after the writer's in every run (the 11-07 run did this from its third large run on).
  • Constrained link: tc netem delay 40ms loss 1% rate 100mbit on the reader's incoming traffic (through an ifb device, so it shapes the direction in which the data arrives), removed after the run.
  • At least two repetitions per scenario; the tables give medians (with two runs, the mean of the two); the single runs are in the raw data.

Limits of this measurement

  • Two repetitions per day, one machine type, two days. Differences of less than about a third between the tools should not be read as real: Resilio Sync's text pull, for example, took 38 s one day and 63 s the next with nothing changed on its side.
  • Resilio Sync 2.8.1 is the last 2.x release; the current stable is 3.1.2, which could not be run this way (below). 3.x may behave differently.
  • Resilio Sync has no tracker or relay here and is given the peers' addresses; Varsto finds them through the bucket. Neither reflects the setup most users have.
  • Varsto's time to propagate a change leaves out its reader's scheduling (the reader is started for the pull; on 11-07 also its detection on the writer); Resilio Sync's includes both. For changes, compare the bytes more than the seconds.
  • Varsto's timed pull also reads the folder's metadata from the bucket (manifests, ledger); Resilio Sync gets the equivalent from its peer. Both are inside the times and the interface bytes.
  • The block volumes are network storage (about 430 MB/s sequential with oflag=direct); random reads of many small files are slow on them for both tools.

Why not Resilio Sync 3.1

The current stable download (stable/linux/x64/0/resilio-sync_x64.tar.gz, "Resilio Sync 3.1.2 (1076)") was tried first, with the same configuration file. It never synced a folder from the file: both ends logged "Stop synchronization" right after loading the folder and every minute after; the reader never opened a connection to its known_hosts entry (no packet to the port in tcpdump), with the read-only or the read-write secret, with known_hosts as a list or a string, with folder_defaults.known_hosts, after creating a user identity (--identity), and also with tracker, relay and LAN search on. use_known_hosts is refused as an unknown key in a shared folder. 2.8.1 with the identical file synced within seconds, so the measurement uses 2.8.1. 3.x presumably wants its folders added and its devices linked through the web interface or its identity; that was not attempted.

Varsto findings of 2026-11-07 (build 46adda3)

These came up while setting the first run up; their status after c50f260 is under "Bug status" above.

  1. With STUN off, a large pull from a peer stops part-way. With varsto p2p enable --no-stun --public <ip>:<port> on the reader (and rendezvous, port mapping, update check, verification, scrub, forwarding and mailbox off), both pulls of the 10 000-file set ended after 65-70 s with 8 846 files written and 1 154 and 1 164 files "chunk ... is not available on any readable storage", all from about f008835 on (the order of the pull). The reader's debug log shows 8 846 /p2p/object requests, all answered 200, and none for the missing blocks: the reader gave up on them without asking the peer. With the same settings but STUN on on the reader, three runs completed (41, 41 and 49 s); with p2p-bench's settings (rendezvous and STUN on) one run completed (75 s). Not root-caused; a guess is that the peer drops out of Peers::ordered() (a route or back-off state) when no STUN answer exists. The results above use STUN on; set BENCH_VARSTO_NO_STUN=1 to reproduce.
  2. A sync right after a peer restarted can fetch nothing. If the reader's service tried the writer while it restarted, it keeps the writer "resting after a failure"; a POST /api/sync in that window (here once in 19 Varsto runs: large, repetition 2) fetched no block and reported every file unavailable instead of waiting or retrying. The driver now restarts the reader's service after the writer's.
  3. The changed-file and rename behaviour above (the pull does not reuse local blocks) is the largest efficiency gap against Resilio Sync.
  4. Every reader run tried every earlier benchmark device as a peer (their device records stay in the vault after the device is gone), up to 17 dead peers per run; each costs a failed TLS handshake at the start.
