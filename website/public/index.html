Why Varsto is different

Bookkeeping you can audit

Every device keeps a signed ledger of what it stored and verified, so Varsto knows how many copies of every block exist, where they are and when another device last checked them. A policy such as "two cloud copies and one at home, verified within 30 days" warns you before it stops being true. Ledger and policies.

Your storage, your keys, no server

A directory, a NAS share, an S3 bucket at any provider or any rclone remote becomes a storage, and a storage ever sees only opaque encrypted objects. There is no Varsto account, server or relay to trust or to lose. Your own storage.

Post-quantum hybrid cryptography

Every ledger batch carries an Ed25519 and an ML-DSA-65 signature, and every folder key travels sealed to an X25519 + ML-KEM-768 key. Designed for a post-quantum world; not audited yet. How the signatures and keys work.

Old disks as counted, verified copies

Plug a disk into a dock and add it to a pool: it fills with the data that has the fewest copies, tells you when it is safe to eject, is recognised when it comes back, and counts toward "one copy at home, one elsewhere". Disk pools.

Strongroom

Folders whose key is wrapped under a secret only your FIDO2 security key produces after a touch, never at rest anywhere. No passphrase, stolen laptop or assistant opens them. Strongroom.

Devices that help each other

Your devices exchange encrypted blocks directly, on the LAN and through NAT, with no vendor relay. A USB stick can carry only what the other side lacks, and a friend's NAS can hold verified copies it cannot read. Peer to peer and transferrers, untrusted replicas.

Where your data can live

Any directory this device can reach, a NAS or network share, any S3-compatible bucket with your own keys (AWS, Scaleway, Hetzner, Backblaze B2, Cloudflare R2, MinIO and others), any rclone remote (SFTP, WebDAV, Google Drive and the rest), cold archive classes that are written but never read without your confirmation, and pools of removable disks on a shelf. Use several at once; each carries a place label and a price, so a policy can ask for one copy outside the house and the app can show what every folder costs a month. Files are cut into blocks, compressed and encrypted on your device, so a storage ever sees only opaque objects and their sizes, never file names, plaintext hashes or content.

Security, honestly

  • Content is encrypted with XChaCha20-Poly1305 under keys derived from a random vault key that only your devices hold; a storage sees ciphertext objects named by their hash and nothing else. How it works, step by step.
  • Every stored signature is hybrid (Ed25519 + ML-DSA-65, both must verify) and every sealed key is hybrid (X25519 + ML-KEM-768): post-quantum hybrid cryptography, designed for, not audited. Transport (direct connections carry only encrypted blocks with a per-request token; QUIC with TLS 1.3 and pinned certificates through NAT) and release signing (minisign) use classical algorithms.
  • Strongroom folders open only with a FIDO2 security key; the enforcement is cryptographic, not a dialog.
  • AI assistance stays local: the MCP server is meant for language models that run on your own machine, inside the folders you grant and never a Strongroom. Nothing is sent to a model provider.
  • A lost device is removed with a signed revocation and the vault moves to new keys; a wipe order runs when the device next reaches a storage. There is no account and no escrow: the recovery kit, 24 words or three shares, is yours to keep.
  • Neither the design nor the code has been reviewed or audited. The threat model and the failure model say what the design intends, what each party can see, and where it stops.

For teams

An organization adds the layer a company needs on top of a vault: administrators approve devices for named people after comparing a six-word fingerprint with them, remove a person with every device of theirs in one step (the remaining devices get new keys), set what members may do on their own, and keep a log of it that is signed by their devices and chained. The root key that appoints administrators is 24 words on paper, never on a device. A vault without an organization sees none of it. Organizations.

What is next

Built on the main branch new in 0.0.1-alpha.14:

  • A vault-wide trash restorable on every device, and version history of files.
  • A second, independent backup with restic: one device keeps a plain mirror of the vault and restic backs it up into a repository outside your storages, restorable with restic alone.
  • Imports from git-annex repositories, cloud drives (through rclone) and local directories, every file checked against its hash.
  • QR invitations and varsto:// links for sharing and pairing.
  • Draining a storage within a daily budget, and storage quotas.
  • A rolling bit-rot scrub of every copy on your storages and disks, with repair from another copy.
  • Router port mapping (PCP, NAT-PMP, UPnP) for peer-to-peer.
  • Folder safety: a mass-deletion guard, send-only and receive-only folders, and ignore rules.

Honest status

  • Released version: 0.0.1-alpha.14. The alpha is a vertical slice: core library, command line, background service, tray and menu-bar shells, Android shell, about 133 000 lines of Rust, 621 automated tests and a lab of 37 scenarios on virtual machines behind home and office NAT: offline convergence, conflicts, a restored-device fence, replicas, sharing, policies, peers, Strongrooms, disk pools, backups and more. Use it with test data only.
  • The cryptographic design has not been reviewed or audited. The threat model and the failure model say what the design intends and where it stops.
  • The source is public at github.com/janttsu/varsto under the PolyForm Shield License 1.0.0 (source-available, not open source); bugs and questions go to the issue tracker there. The storage format is documented so that your data stays recoverable.
  • Varsto is written in Rust, with Swift and Kotlin shells and a plain web interface. Security review, platform work, network and storage testing, packaging and translations are all welcome: how to contribute.
  • The name is provisional.

Downloads

No binaries are published yet. Build from source with cargo build --release.

Verify a download: the checksum list is signed with the Varsto release key (see "Verify your download" above): varsto verify-release <file> --github, or minisign and sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS --ignore-missing. varsto update checks the signature and the GitHub release by itself.
