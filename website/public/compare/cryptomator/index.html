Varsto and Cryptomator

Cryptomator encrypts a folder, the vault, so that it can be kept in any cloud without the provider reading it. It is free and open source, has desktop and mobile apps, and does one thing: the vault. Getting the vault to another device is left to the cloud provider's own client.

How Cryptomator works

A vault is a directory of encrypted files and directories with a vault.cryptomator configuration file at its root. On the desktop the vault is shown as a virtual drive: files are encrypted and decrypted transparently as you use them, and there are no unencrypted copies on disk. File names are encrypted too. Content is encrypted with AES (the SIV_GCM or SIV_CTRMAC combination), and the master keys are protected with a password through scrypt and AES key wrap. Cryptomator does not move the vault anywhere: you put it in a folder that Dropbox, Google Drive, OneDrive or another client synchronises, and that client carries the encrypted files between devices. There is no record of which devices or storages hold a copy, no sharing mechanism of its own (whoever has the vault folder and its password opens it; a separate Hub product manages access for organisations), and no rules about how many copies must exist.

Which one fits

Cryptomator

Choose Cryptomator when you already use a cloud client you are happy with and want its folder encrypted with a mature, open-source tool, and you do not need to know where copies are or to share with others.

Varsto

Choose Varsto when the encryption should come with the sync itself, across buckets, NAS shares and removable disks, with a record of where every copy is, policies that warn when a copy is missing, and sharing with other people.

Varsto is alpha software: do not trust it with data you have no other copy of, and its cryptography has not been independently audited. The other products here are mature.
