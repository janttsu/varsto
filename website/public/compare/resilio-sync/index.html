Varsto and Resilio Sync

Resilio Sync syncs and shares folders between devices peer to peer, built on BitTorrent technology. The free Sync Home edition is for individuals; the company's main business is enterprise file transfer. Resilio was acquired by Nasuni in March 2026.

Where Varsto differs

  • Source-available, with a published storage format: your data stays recoverable without the vendor.
  • Hybrid post-quantum cryptography for every stored signature and every sealed key (designed for; not audited).
  • Your storages are the always-available copy, so no always-on peer is needed; devices still exchange blocks directly when they can reach each other.
  • A signed ledger counts and verifies every copy, and durability policies warn before a rule breaks.
  • No vendor trackers or relays: devices find each other through your storages and on the LAN, and relay through one of your own devices when NAT cannot be punched.

How Resilio Sync works

Devices connect directly; there is no account, and folders are shared with keys or links (read-only or read-write). Resilio-run trackers and relays help peers find each other. Data lives on the peers only; Sync Home has no S3 or rclone backend. Selective sync with placeholder files is available, and an "encrypted folder" lets a peer on an untrusted machine store data it cannot read (that peer is read-only and has no selective sync).

Measured against Resilio Sync (November 2026)

On 8 November 2026 both tools moved the same data between the same three rented machines (a writer in Paris, readers in Amsterdam and Warsaw, 2 vCPU each), one tool at a time, with relays and trackers off, and every file was checked by SHA-256 afterwards. Varsto was the daily build 20261108-c50f260; Resilio Sync was 2.8.1, because 3.1.2 did not sync from a configuration file at all. Medians of two runs. "Bytes" is what the reader exchanged with the other machines. The method, its limits, the raw data and the scripts to repeat it are in the measurement report.

Green marks a row where Varsto was faster.

  • Varsto is faster in every initial sync of large, compressible or duplicated data, and over the slow lossy link. Text costs a third of the bytes, because blocks are compressed before they are encrypted; a renamed directory and duplicate files move almost no data, because blocks already on the reader's disk are reused.
  • Resilio Sync is faster on many small files when it does not stall (it did in one run of two), and at propagating a small change: Varsto now fetches only the changed blocks (22 MB instead of the whole 1.1 GiB in the build before), but still takes three to five times longer, because the writer's own sync of all folders and the reader's re-hashing of its local blocks cost more than the transfer.
  • Two readers finished in about the same time with both tools; Varsto's readers fed each other in one run of two, and its writer still sent more.
  • Memory: Varsto's writer service peaked at 360–800 MiB against Resilio Sync's 70–250 MiB.

One day, one provider, two runs per case, and an alpha: these are indications, not a verdict. The report lists everything that limits them.

Which one fits

Resilio Sync

Choose Resilio Sync when you want fast BitTorrent-style transfer between always-on machines and easy sharing by key or link, and a closed-source vendor product is acceptable.

Varsto

Varsto's peer-to-peer transfer is modelled on the same idea (encrypted blocks from several peers at once), but it adds your own storages as the always-available copy, a record of where every copy is, durability policies and an open specification of the formats.

Varsto is alpha software: do not trust it with data you have no other copy of, and its cryptography has not been independently audited. The other products here are mature. Facts about them were checked from their own documentation in October 2026; prices and plans change often, so see their own pages for current figures. Corrections are welcome as GitHub issues.
